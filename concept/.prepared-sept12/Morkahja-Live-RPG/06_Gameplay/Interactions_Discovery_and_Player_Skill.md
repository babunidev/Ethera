# Interactions Discovery and Player Skill

An interaction is available or unavailable; multiple enabling sources do not strengthen it, and the tooltip lists all reasons. Capable AI pauses routine work to cooperate. Atranor failure is terminal, unlike normal retries.

**Current concept through answer 202 · updated 2026-09-12.**

Consolidated concept material, with current rules and unresolved details distinguished.

## Interaction System

### Shared vocabulary

Persistent Desire ↔ Trait and situational Need ↔ Shard pairings create meaningful opportunities. Relevant people, creatures, clothing, tools, weapons, items, places, and structures can carry traits. Permanent personal qualities and changeable world/equipment states have different lifetimes.

Relationships, roles, proximity, equipment, and injuries shape usability. Ordinary baseline actions exist without a special pair for each movement or touch. A character may desire a trait they already possess.

### Local exchanges and social growth

Character-to-character exchanges occur nearby and must be performed. Knowledge does not synchronize by proximity. Recognition, sympathy, and trust can develop through compatible interactions; neutral and positive social states are sufficient for the first version.

### Possibility versus execution

Compatibility primarily unlocks actions, including conditional capabilities supplied by equipment. It does not secretly improve success-roll percentages. Character qualities determine what is possible; player skill determines how well the action is performed.

Skill is designed per interaction: timing, observation, memory, pattern recognition, sequencing, or selecting a suitable response can matter. This is not a rule that all actions become quick-time events. Generation can remain random while execution is reliable and understandable.

### Discovery and presentation

Unknown interactions remain completely unhinted until revealed through ordinary play. Discovered interactions persist in the character panel with explanations and visible blockers when unusable. In-world actions use the hold-focus radial menu and subsequent skill phase; no separate task-acquisition dashboard is required.

### Failure

Failure changes circumstances and may create a new Need, but never permanently closes the route. Each Need must have a possible solution. Detailed recovery algorithms remain open; generated chains must not loop back into unresolved Needs.

See [Interaction Discovery](#interaction-discovery), [Combat](Combat_and_Injuries.md), and [Crafting](../07_Items/Crafting_and_Item_Traits.md).

## Interaction Discovery

### Discover through play

Interactions can become available when the character's qualities and the actual situation fit. Discovery comes through quests, exploration, equipment use, meeting people, combat, and communal work. It is not designed as a combination-testing laboratory.

Before discovery, an interaction has no preview, silhouette, remaining-count hint, or suggested combination. Players learn it exists when the situation reveals it.

### Permanent character record

After discovery, the interaction remains documented in the character panel. The panel explains which traits, role, equipment, relationship, or conditions enable it.

If temporarily unavailable, it stays visible and clearly identifies the missing ingredient or blocking condition. Losing equipment or receiving an injury changes usability, not the record of discovery. The record belongs to the persistent character rather than granting account-owned power.

### In play versus in the panel

In-world contextual inputs perform actions directly, with cultural or physical animation. The panel provides optional explanation and flavor, not a requirement to inspect formulas during play.

Right click moves and hold-left-click focuses; exact quick keys and the discovery trigger remain open: the conversation ties discovery to encountering the revealing circumstances but does not define a separate mandatory successful-use test.

See [Interaction System](#interaction-system) and [Diegetic Interface](../08_Presentation/Interface_and_Divine_Orientation.md).

## Focus, contextual choices, and automatic positioning

Right click sets a movement destination. Hover can change the cursor but does not slow the character. Hold left click on an interactable target to focus: the character slows and a radial menu opens. Drag toward an action and release, or use its displayed quick key. Choosing it sets a new intention; the character automatically moves into range and faces correctly.

The radial menu shows only discovered interactions executable in the current situation, using traits, carried/equipped items, role, target, and condition. No disabled options or alternate-equipment subpage. Known blocked actions remain inspectable with reasons in the character panel.

Slots fill dynamically rather than preserving fixed action positions. Attention to today's available actions is intentional. Assistant illustration: a tree could offer Climb/Search bark/Collect resin on one visit and Inspect damage/Reach trapped animal/Cut branch on another. These are examples, not a required action catalogue.

Focus slows the participant rather than world time. The discussion also proposed slowing both participants during combat selection and using animation for a slow-motion feeling; multiplayer interruption, third-party attacks, cancellation, and limits are unresolved. The initial hover-trigger and time-bubble ideas were superseded by deliberate holding and participant pacing.

## Small skill phases and immediate retries

Most or all interactions should include a second player-skill phase after selection and positioning. It need not be hard: timing, observation, sequencing, a small lock puzzle, pushing an object, adjusting an angle, or setting a lever can establish deliberate participation. A combat example is a closing ring around a cursor showing the mouse button to press before it reaches the center. Exact interfaces and exceptions remain to be designed.

The phase belongs to the action, not a random success roll. Assistant illustrations include aligning pieces, holding pressure, following vibration patterns, and choosing a response to another character's movement.

Failure has already cost time and may cause injury or change the target. Recheck availability; if the interaction is still possible, retry immediately. No artificial retry cooldown. Falling and injuring a leg may remove climbing; an unchanged lock may permit another attempt. These are consequences and examples, not a universal injury rule for every failed action.
