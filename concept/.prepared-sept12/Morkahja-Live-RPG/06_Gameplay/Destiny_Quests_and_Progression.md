# Destiny Quests and Progression

**Current concept through answer 202 · updated 2026-09-12.**

## Destiny

### Continuing Crests and Knots

Destiny begins at the adulthood rite. A **Destiny Crest** starts with Combat, Trickery, Aid, and Social approaches, branches outward, then converges inward toward one **Destiny Knot**. A Knot is the culmination of a Crest, not a terminal destiny after which the person has nothing left to pursue. Life can continue through many Crests and Knots.

The core purpose of a Knot remains central. Its preparation, ritual components, required items, and other Needs can change with the person's traits, equipment, and developed approaches. Obtaining a spear may add a spear-related task; losing it can change that component. This revises the old framing of one core purpose per Destiny Knot, followed by further Crests for the entire life.

One branch can reach and complete the Knot. All four are not mandatory. A player may instead develop other branches before completing it, enriching the culmination. Dominant current traits and branch development shape its expression: an Aid-oriented person may still have an Aid-colored Knot after developing several branches, while new combat traits and experience can shift it toward fighting. The exact scoring of dominance is open.

### Community culmination and continued life

The Knot brings learning and experience back to the village through a major ritual, a sequence of Needs, a reveal, and a communal celebration. Detailed Grimnir ceremony design remains open. It is more than a final combat encounter or a menu reward.

The next Crest begins immediately, without mandatory downtime. The player may see the immediate next step or sense continuation before arrival, but the next branching structure reveals itself when reached. No complete future-life diagram is exposed. Branches remain switchable; completed branch progress is not erased by trying another approach.

Only players advance personal destiny. Reproduction temporarily replaces parents' ordinary destiny with Reproduction Destinies; see [Birth and youth](../03_Characters/Birth_Youth_and_Adulthood.md). AI rules for that exception are unresolved.

## Objectives and Side Quests

### Present character, evolving opportunities

Generate from current Traits, Desires, and community Needs: who the person is now and what future participation requires. Do not add a separate biography-scoring system. Past experience can already be embodied in current traits and relationships.

Fill the first quest slots, then create subsequent steps from current conditions and the preceding outcomes. An active quest can gain preparatory Needs or detours. If another person has a required item, obtaining it becomes a step; their requested exchange can lead onward to another person, resource, or preparation. Keep this readable: quests give direction and accomplishment while interaction and player freedom remain the experience.

Use existing items and completed deeds where fitting. A previously acquired object can satisfy an applicable later Need; do not spawn a duplicate simply because a quest was accepted. Distinct consumptive Needs require enough separate supply, and each Need must be completed separately.

### Rewards

Knot completion should provide at least one lasting Trait, generally connected to the approach that shaped it. Developing several approaches may yield several Traits, perhaps one per meaningful direction; exact thresholds and counts are not fixed. The Knot is analogous to a level-up without numeric levels.

An external item or Shard can also be a reward; a vision or revelation can become knowledge to bring to the community. The precise external reward pool and guarantee are not settled. Ordinary quests do not require a permanent Trait or item payout. They can provide progress, discovery, opportunities to fulfill a Desire, temporary vitality or a buff. A feeling of moving faster after success was an example, not a fixed statistic. Minimize unnecessary reward-object generation.

### Player priority

Select an existing task or quest thread in the character panel, marked by a small indicator, possibly a marker stone. Divine Orientation follows one relevant Need within that thread at a time and may choose the nearest sensible next one. On completion, the game can suggest the oldest unresolved thread, with oldest personal Need first and community fallback. The player can always change priority. New task acceptance remains an in-world cultural-source interaction.

## Procedural Destiny System

### Solvability and independent completion

Every Need has a fitting attainable fulfillment. If the capability is missing, a preparatory route must supply the means. Failure can change circumstances and create new Needs, but does not permanently close the route.

No manufactured contradictory requirements: preserving one object and breaking another are separate solutions, not an imposed impossible choice over the same unique object. If two Needs require meat, provide sufficient separate portions. Shared places and knowledge may still connect lives, but completion states remain independent.

Dependency chains have no arbitrary depth cap, but cannot loop back into an unresolved prerequisite. For A → B → C → A, change C's fulfillment. Generation must preserve reachability through evolving ownership and availability; technical checks and concurrent allocation remain implementation work.

### World connection

Crests and tasks use persistent nodes and content slots in the expanding hex-tile world. The rough direction of adding terrain per quest must coexist with existing-object continuity. Shards carry observed history; the world node carries present state. Mortal knowledge updates only through actual exchange, while Divine Orientation may know the current valid route.

See [World nodes and terrain](../01_World/World_Nodes_and_Generation.md), [Community tasks](../04_Community/Settlement_Simulation_and_Tasks.md), and [Trade](../07_Items/Trade_and_Exchange.md).

*Illustrative example from the discussion.*

## Defined Crest shape and meaningful choices

The chosen board structure is 4 → 8 → 16 → 8 → 4 → 1 Destiny Knot. Each broad direction can offer choices within itself: an initial Combat quest opens two different Combat opportunities, later widening before reconvergence. Switching to Trickery, Aid, or Social remains possible if the remaining options are less appealing.

Generate alternatives from the person's current Traits and Desires, using distinct traits or combinations where available. With few combat traits, two quests may be variations of one activity type involving different creatures rather than fabricated new capabilities. Branching supplies choice, not automatic greater mechanical complexity.

Complete prerequisites to unlock the next visible slots. Reaching the Knot through one route remains sufficient; developing other routes can enrich it. The exact merge prerequisite semantics must preserve this rule rather than requiring completion of the whole graph. Personal extra Needs live in the common slot, and quest objectives remain in their quests. Whether the role receives separate generation weight is unanswered Question 203.
