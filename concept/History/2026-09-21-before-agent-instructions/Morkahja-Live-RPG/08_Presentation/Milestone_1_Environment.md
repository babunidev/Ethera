# Milestone 1 Environment

A small low-poly Grimnir village center provides the first playable place. Low stone structures partially enclose an open gathering space. The Memory Fountain stands at its heart as a separate object, surrounded by room to walk, approach and interact.

## Continuous shaped stone

Grimnir shape and merge stone into a continuous mass. Walls flow into supports, seats and shallow alcoves. Broad, deliberate facets describe rounded, organic silhouettes, with smooth transitions between architectural functions and the underlying terrain.

The result feels patiently formed and cared for. Its surfaces communicate flowing stone and subtle wear from use. Forms remain grounded, modest and readable from the gameplay camera.

## Ground and circulation

Uneven exposed bedrock blends into compacted earth, scattered gravel and worn footpaths. Paths converge on the Fountain space. The central placement area stays relatively level, while the surrounding terrain retains natural variation.

Reserve clear walking and interaction space for Khar-Morr, the Rock + Shapeable object and the loose club. Keep approach positions usable beside the rock and Fountain. The environment and the Fountain remain separate assets during modeling and scene assembly.

## Seats, vegetation and light

A few seats and resting ledges are shaped into the surrounding stone. Sparse grass and small patches of moss occupy sheltered cracks. Warm daylight and open sky keep the center clear, calm and intimate.

The overall feeling is grounded and gently magical: an early communal place formed through patient care. Environmental presentation supports the character, world objects and skill graphics as one visible game space.

## Modeling and assembly

Build the terrain patch, continuous stone surroundings, integrated seats, ground-material variation and restrained vegetation in Blender. Place the existing Fountain asset in the reserved center during Godot scene assembly. Broad facets and clear silhouettes guide detail density; dimensions and polygon counts are practical tuning choices.
