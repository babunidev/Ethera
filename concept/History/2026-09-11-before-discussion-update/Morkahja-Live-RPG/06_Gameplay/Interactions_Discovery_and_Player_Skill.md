# Interactions Discovery and Player Skill

**Current concept through answer 104 · reorganized 2026-09-10.**

Consolidated concept material, with current rules and unresolved details distinguished.

## Interaction System

### Shared vocabulary

Persistent Desire ↔ Trait and situational Need ↔ Shard pairings create meaningful opportunities. Relevant people, creatures, clothing, tools, weapons, items, places, and structures can carry traits. Permanent personal qualities and changeable world/equipment states have different lifetimes.

Relationships, roles, proximity, equipment, and injuries shape usability. Ordinary baseline actions exist without a special pair for each movement or touch. A character may desire a trait they already possess.

### Local exchanges and social growth

Character-to-character exchanges occur nearby and must be performed. Knowledge does not synchronize by proximity. Recognition, sympathy, and trust can develop through compatible interactions; neutral and positive social states are sufficient for the first version.

### Possibility versus execution

Compatibility primarily unlocks actions, including conditional capabilities supplied by equipment. It does not secretly improve success-roll percentages. Character qualities determine what is possible; player skill determines how well the action is performed.

Skill is designed per interaction: timing, observation, memory, pattern recognition, sequencing, or selecting a suitable response can matter. This is not a rule that all actions become quick-time events. Generation can remain random while execution is reliable and understandable.

### Discovery and presentation

Unknown interactions remain completely unhinted until revealed through ordinary play. Discovered interactions persist in the character panel with explanations and visible blockers when unusable. In-world actions use direct contextual inputs; no operating menu must open first.

### Failure

Failure changes circumstances and may create a new Need, but never permanently closes the route. Each Need must have a possible solution. Detailed recovery and cyclic dependency rules remain open.

See [Interaction Discovery](#interaction-discovery), [Combat](Combat_and_Injuries.md), and [Crafting](../07_Items/Crafting_and_Item_Traits.md).

## Interaction Discovery

### Discover through play

Interactions can become available when the character's qualities and the actual situation fit. Discovery comes through quests, exploration, equipment use, meeting people, combat, and communal work. It is not designed as a combination-testing laboratory.

Before discovery, an interaction has no preview, silhouette, remaining-count hint, or suggested combination. Players learn it exists when the situation reveals it.

### Permanent character record

After discovery, the interaction remains documented in the character panel. The panel explains which traits, role, equipment, relationship, or conditions enable it.

If temporarily unavailable, it stays visible and clearly identifies the missing ingredient or blocking condition. Losing equipment or receiving an injury changes usability, not the record of discovery. The record belongs to the persistent character rather than granting account-owned power.

### In play versus in the panel

In-world contextual inputs perform actions directly, with cultural or physical animation. The panel provides optional explanation and flavor, not a requirement to inspect formulas during play.

Specific bindings and the exact discovery trigger remain open: the conversation ties discovery to encountering the revealing circumstances but does not define a separate mandatory successful-use test.

See [Interaction System](#interaction-system) and [Diegetic Interface](../08_Presentation/Interface_and_Divine_Orientation.md).

## Example E41: A sword changes the action set

*Illustrative example from the discussion.*

A compatible sword can expose a conditional capability like Sword Handling or “one with a sword.” It opens interactions rather than secretly changing a hit percentage. The person’s capabilities decide available actions; the player’s execution decides success.

## Example E44: Different kinds of skill

*Illustrative example from the discussion.*

Timing is appropriate for some responses. Observation, memory, pattern recognition, sequencing, or choosing a contextual action can matter elsewhere. The user left each interaction’s test to its own design rather than making the game a string of timing events.
