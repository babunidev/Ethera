# Destiny Quests and Progression

**Current concept through answer 104 · reorganized 2026-09-10.**

Personal purpose, four approaches, generated objectives, rewards, solvability, and recovery belong to one connected design.

## Destiny

### One purpose, multiple approaches

Destiny begins at the adulthood ritual alongside the revealed role. It unfolds gradually through quests. The final purpose is initially hidden, then revealed through progress.

Its structure branches outward into variation and later converges on one clear final purpose. Combat, Trickery, Aid, and Social are the four broad approaches. The example branch counts discussed are illustrative, not an approved fixed graph.

The final purpose does not change when a different approach is followed, but the path, allies, knowledge, and details of the final quest can change.

### Switchable and combinatorial

Branches remain available rather than disappearing when another is chosen. They may progress unevenly; switching to a less-developed approach continues from its current stage. Branch progress is not a visible level or power rating.

Traits, desires, equipment, shards, roles, and current capabilities determine which quests can be meaningfully attempted. New rewards broaden future possibilities. A quest may combine several of the four approaches when the character supports them.

Only present opportunities and limited immediate next steps are known; future nodes and solutions are hidden. Later interface clarification places decisions in world actions rather than a branch-selection dashboard. Branch freedom remains, but role inhabitation and immersion govern how it is exercised.

### Failure and changing routes

Failure never permanently closes a quest or interaction route. Changed circumstances create Needs and recoverable detours. Every generated Need must have a possible solution, using existing reality or generated content.

Exchange dependencies may extend without a fixed depth cap. These runtime detours coexist with the overarching converging destiny structure.

### Purpose and continuity

Only players advance destiny. Purposeful pursuit can sustain energy; community participation also supports integrity. Atranor and the larger metaphysical dangers remain, with their precise relationship to recoverable quest failure still open.

See [Objectives and Side Quests](#objectives-and-side-quests) and [Procedural Destiny System](#procedural-destiny-system).

## Objectives and Side Quests

### Goals and available capabilities

An objective states the needed outcome, such as acquiring an existing claw, rather than requiring repeated kills. Existing items and prior accomplishment can satisfy a later requirement. Ownership changes alter the route, not the object's existence.

Quests normally use the character's current traits, role, equipment, and available interactions. Rewards expand what subsequent quests can ask. If a required capability is missing, obtaining the means must be part of the quest rather than an impossible demand.

### Branching and complexity

Combat, Trickery, Aid, and Social branches can be followed in different orders and combined as capabilities broaden. The larger structure expands then converges on one fixed final purpose; approach changes its details. Do not treat example branch counts as fixed templates.

Complexity stays hidden. Players see the next situation, not tiers, power recommendations, or a full solution checklist. Unrevealed interactions and future paths remain hidden until normal play exposes them.

### Rewards and recovery

Activity-related traits remain permanent rewards. Broader possibilities can include new information, equipment, relationships, and discovered actions. Reward catalogues are not fixed.

Failure never permanently closes a route. It creates changed circumstances and possible new Needs. Every Need must have at least one possible solution; link an existing one or generate it. Exchange chains may grow without an artificial depth cap.

### Community work and UI

Community tasks are separate simulation-generated work, with deliberate cooperation and adaptive alternatives when another person declines. Accept them through direct cultural interactions at the knowledge fountain.

The character panel explains known state; it is not a recurring task/branch-selection dashboard. Branch freedom remains in world actions, within the life the player chose to inhabit.

See [Community Tasks](../04_Community/Settlement_Simulation_and_Tasks.md), [Destiny](#destiny), and [Trade and Exchange](../07_Items/Trade_and_Exchange.md).

## Procedural Destiny System

### Character development pipeline

Birth establishes personal trait and desire foundations → early childhood is simulated → youth becomes playable with elemental baseline → participation reveals hidden youth traits/desires → coming-of-age quest and ritual → role revealed and destiny begins.

The 27-trait 9+9+9 origin remains, with no category balancing. Unmet local Desires can prioritize matching traits in future newborns; exact weighting is not specified.

### Destiny shape

The quest web opens into Combat, Trickery, Aid, and Social approaches, varies, then converges on a single final purpose. Branches remain switchable and can progress unevenly. The approach changes circumstances, not the final purpose.

Only current and limited next opportunities are revealed. Internal complexity follows combinations of available capabilities; there are no player-facing tiers. Fixed node counts or generation stages are not approved.

### Solvability and expansion

Generate quests from current abilities, equipment, and interactions. If something required is missing, create an attainable preparatory path. Every created Need has a possible solution in current or newly generated content.

Failure changes the situation and creates Needs without permanently closing a route. Item exchanges may add uncapped dependency chains. Recovery of destroyed objects or vanished participants must preserve possibility, though exact algorithms remain open.

### World and social context

Destinies reuse suitable content slots and add new world content. Nodes keep current state; shards preserve knowledge history. Unmet Desires can influence local content or future people, without an immediate guarantee for social counterparts.

Deliberate cooperative work primarily belongs to continuously generated community tasks. A declined invitation triggers an alternate partner or task adaptation.

See [World Nodes](../01_World/World_Nodes_and_Generation.md), [Community Tasks](../04_Community/Settlement_Simulation_and_Tasks.md), and [Destiny](#destiny).
