# Social Roles

**Status:** Established concept, updated through answer 104 on 2026-09-10; later user corrections take precedence.

Roles are culture-specific callings assigned at adulthood from a person's traits. The four peoples begin separately and do not use one universal class list. The definitive role list is intentionally deferred.

## Mechanical meaning

A role grants special abilities, acts, knowledge, and unique interactions. It can change what a character perceives. The explicit example is a hunter who can track prey and see tracks in the environment.

Certain roles can unlock interactions with another person, or shared actions for both participants. The comparison to Dota heroes concerns distinct capabilities, not player selection of a fixed hero build.

## Narrative meaning

Role helps shape destiny and side quests. Some rewards should reinforce that calling while still broadening the person. A hunter's story should make sense for their abilities and culture rather than feel randomly unrelated.

## Cultural foundations

The broader lore includes Rokur smithing and communal work; Verdani healing, waterkeeping, and gardening; Aevis learning, architecture, and crystal craft; Grimnir forging, architecture, navigation, and memorykeeping. These provide foundations, not an approved ancient-era playable class roster.


## Update — 2026-09-10

Roles modify an existing humanoid and elemental action baseline; ordinary action is not locked behind a role. Traits, Desires, Needs, Shards, equipment, and injuries together determine meaningful interactions. Role-based activity may also influence emerging Desires, with youth development completed before the adulthood rite. See [Interaction System](../06_Systems/Interaction_System.md).


## Revelation and flat craft traits

The adulthood ritual reveals the role; youths do not preview their adult calling. Inhabiting an adult means accepting that role for the session, with world actions rather than activity menus.

Crafter, Leatherworker, and Cloak Crafter are independent traits, not a hierarchical promotion ladder. Relevant overlapping craft qualities contribute together. Examples of hunters, mothers, shamans, and craftspeople illustrate inhabitation; the initial Grimnir role roster is not fixed.
