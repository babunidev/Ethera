# Art, Audio, Camera and Movement

Morkahja has a restrained, serious fantasy atmosphere: ancient, strange and quietly dreamlike. Stylization leaves room for imagination, while small movements, sounds and changes in light reward attention.

## Visual direction

The first Grimnir community is discovering individual and shared life among stone and wilderness. Broad grassland, savanna and hills create space around modest shelters, work areas and sacred places.

Grimnir bodies have layered mineral plates, luminous ore and crystal seams, and gemstone eyes. Shaped stone, purposeful joins and resonant material give their surroundings a related visual language. Later Skylands extend it into curved bridges, leaning forms and crystal lattices scattering light.

Magic can appear as subtle distortion or energy flowing into and out of terrain and living things. The world remains calm enough for these details to be noticed.

## People and possessions

Bodies communicate condition through posture, movement, brightness, solidity and definition. Well-being can make a person more vivid; weakening can dim or blur their form.

Equipment, bags, bundles and clothes change the silhouette. The oldest active visible state leads the animation, while every active state continues to affect mechanics and appears in the character panel.

## Sound and communication

Stone contact, shaping sounds, low hums, tool rhythms and shared resonance give Grimnir life its sound. Music, gatherings and cultural stories bring communal activity into the environment.

Players communicate through contextual actions, gestures, movement and knowledge exchange. Player text and voice chat are absent. Authored character sound and environmental storytelling belong to the world's own expression.

## Movement

Right click sets a destination. Movement is direct, precise and physical. During interaction, the character approaches the target and faces it. Combat engagement maintains natural orientation and positioning; disengagement returns independent movement.

## Camera

An elevated angled view keeps the person and surrounding details visible. The player can freely orbit the character, look back along a route and retain the chosen angle as they move.

Zoom ranges from close observation to a broader sense of scale. The camera stays anchored to the character. Spectators choose an existing on-screen person to follow.
