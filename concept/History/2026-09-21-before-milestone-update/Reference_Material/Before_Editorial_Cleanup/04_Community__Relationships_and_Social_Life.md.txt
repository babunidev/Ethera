# Relationships and Social Life

**Current concept through answer 251 · updated 2026-09-13.**

Consolidated concept material, with current rules and unresolved details distinguished.

## Relationships

### Proximity and compatibility

Relationships develop through meaningful interactions between physically nearby characters. preference Traits match Traits, and Needs match Shards; roles and existing relationship information also shape what is available.

A character who preferences Strong can connect with someone who is Strong. An initial greeting or culturally appropriate action can create sympathy shards, enabling further interactions later. Receiving needed information can also deepen the relationship.

### Positive progression

The intended progression is recognition → sympathy → trust, with culturally appropriate bonds developing through repeated matched interactions. These are relationship states or shards rather than a required numeric friendship bar; exact thresholds are not defined.

The first version uses neutral and positive relationships. There is no hatred, rivalry, aversion, or negative reputation simulation. If compatibility is absent, deeper positive interaction simply remains unavailable.

### Community and reproduction

Players create circumstances in which people can meet each other's needs and preferences, strengthening the community. Social connection ultimately supports partnership and reproduction and thus population growth. This is an overall social-system purpose, not a rule that every exchange directly causes reproduction.

A character may desire a trait they also possess. For Grimnir, community care replaces an exclusive family network. Creator origins can be historical knowledge; inheritance of specific relationship Shards remains undefined.

Permanent traits are not erased by ordinary social changes. Temporary relationship knowledge is distinct from the persistent trait pool.

See [preference Traits](../03_Characters/Identity_Traits_Desires_and_Needs.md) and [Social Gameplay](#social-gameplay).

## Social Gameplay

Roleplay has practical meaning. Shared work, team activity, music, dance, celebration, and communal moments can make people healthier, more resilient, and more grounded in their individuality.

The intended audience is adult, and adult sexuality and intimate relationships were included as part of social life. Their presentation and mechanics are not specified. Early childhood is simulated; youth is the separate playable formation phase, with age-appropriate interaction details still to be defined.

### Contextual interaction

Interactions arise from the actual relationship between two people, their roles, traits, and matching pieces of knowledge. The design should ask what meaningful exchange exists here rather than offer only a shallow universal gesture list.

If one character needs a bear's location and another knows it, a knowledge-sharing action becomes possible. Culture determines how the exchange appears. Other matching Trait combinations can unlock special actions for one or both participants.

There is no free player text or proximity voice chat. Signals, contextual actions, movement, and culturally appropriate exchanges carry communication.

Social opportunities require physical proximity and meaningful matching: preference Trait ↔ Trait or Need ↔ Shard. Executing an interaction can create relationship shards and unlock further bonding, from recognition to sympathy to trust. Neutral and positive states are sufficient; hatred, rivalry, and negative reputation are excluded from the initial design.

preference Traits extend beyond social life because creatures, objects, and places have traits too. Specific matches should support varied actions, beginning with a manageable cultural interaction library. Ordinary greetings and movement also have a baseline, so not every basic action needs a specialized personal trait. See [Relationships](#relationships) and [Interaction System](../06_Gameplay/Interactions_Discovery_and_Player_Skill.md).

## Knowledge exchange, family cooperation, and unconditional belonging

Exchange Knowledge is a cultural conversation about lived events: what someone made, discovered, or knows about an object's current holder. Information still spreads through performed interactions. Exact preference Trait–Trait matches preserve the significance of finding a compatible person.

Grimnir affection can include matching a companion's resting resonance. Two willing adults can initiate Reproduction Destinies with individual and shared tasks; a stable nest allows them to travel. Care is not repayment for labor: people belong while young, exhausted, injured, recovering, or unable to work.

## Exact pairwise bonds that remain

Relations are specific to two Grimnir, not a global reputation from social Traits. One exact preference Trait–Trait pair enables a Recognition interaction; two enable the Sympathy step; three enable Trust. The matching can run in either direction between the people. Compatibility alone does not advance the relation: perform the relevant interaction. Recognition precedes Sympathy, then Trust.

Examples: Strong matches a desire for Strong; a greeting or bow establishes Recognition; warmer dancing or affectionate/flirty interaction can express Sympathy; appropriate adult closeness and reproductive interaction can become available with Trust. Final animations and age-specific boundaries remain to be designed.

Once established, a stage is permanent: no decay, jealousy, betrayal meter, or backward progression. Trust is the highest general emotional stage. Losing a temporary compatibility source does not undo the bond; availability of particular actions is still evaluated from current conditions.

Grimnir society has no required exclusive couples or nuclear-family ladder. Trusted individuals may initiate creation with different trusted partners. Parenthood is not a fourth relationship rank.


Source: [answer 180](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-cef1b54a-dbfe-4525-b460-541cd0c6187a), [answer 181](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-cfcf65c3-3a8f-44e4-b8e5-15d22af254da), [answer 182](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-a9fd8814-14e6-458c-b39e-45527fe0ae67).


## Helpful exchange and social tasks

Capable AI members pause ordinary work to assist. An exchange can ask for an item, knowledge, assistance, reassurance, or an appropriate social interaction, including a hug or adult affection where compatibility and relationship permit. It is fulfillment of Needs/preference Traits, not only item-for-item barter. No automatic consent is imposed on player-controlled partners.

Community tasks can be entirely social: dances, resonance gatherings, storytelling, feasts, welcoming, rest, and celebration preparation are examples. Participants are selected by required qualities—Traits, Needs, and other relevant compatibility—not arbitrary 'any three villagers.' A feast may need a cook and people who are hungry. If ideal people do not exist, make a smaller valid activity using available people rather than demanding nonexistent participants.


Source: [answer 163](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-a92dcfd6-cd0d-4df4-bff6-0e4071d78a88), [answer 186](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-1d373e21-38ed-4f69-9494-a4ead9d66cf5), [answer 187](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-47b1873c-4101-42a5-a769-fa2a8c8b8465), [answer 188](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-b9f1b22e-a1a8-4aeb-bef9-aad8eb5cc54c).


## Learn identity through exchange and recall shared history

An inhabited player sees their own expressed Traits and may inspect character details in spectator/selection mode; they do not inspect everyone else's hidden character sheet in the world. People have no overhead names, role labels, health bars, Trait lists or relationship icons. Clothing, items, gait, posture and behavior convey visible condition; hover offers subtle interaction cues.

Exchange Knowledge can teach who someone is, such as their name. Relationship depth and compatible knowledge/Needs affect available information and interactions. Having information does not plaster it over the character's head.

Recall Knowledge is a contextual action on a person. It opens a small stone disk showing the relationship and knowledge this character actually has: Shards about that person, Shards learned from them, and what they learned from this character. Track who taught whom; this is a relationship-specific shared history, not an omniscient biography. The cursor may subtly differ for Recognition, Sympathy and Trust, without overhead icons.

Routine shared history keeps a recent period rather than an endless transcript. Important lasting facts remain: creating a child together, crafting a very rare item, performing a rare ritual, an intimate moment or romance. These facts can be authored prerequisites for later interactions alongside Traits, Needs, items, Shards and state. There is no automatic generic relationship-bonus engine.

History retention does not delete functional personal Shards, regress permanent Recognition/Sympathy/Trust, introduce exclusive couples, or restore permanent parent roles. The duration of the recent window, the lasting-fact catalogue and exact disk layout remain open. After the vocabulary simplification, a matching pair is a preference Trait such as Likes Strong and the counterpart Strong; the established one/two/three-pair progression remains unless later revised.
