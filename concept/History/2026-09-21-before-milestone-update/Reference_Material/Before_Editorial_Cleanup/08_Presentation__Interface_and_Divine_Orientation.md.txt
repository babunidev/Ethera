# Interface and Divine Orientation

**Current concept through answer 251 · updated 2026-09-13.**

What the player can inspect, how actions are performed directly, and how divine signs guide without becoming a constant pointer.

## Diegetic Interface

### Explain the person; act in the world

The major experience choice happens when inhabiting a character. The player accepts that person's role and then lives it through world actions. The UI explains identity and known possibilities and permits prioritizing existing tasks; physical actions are chosen through contextual focus.

Bodies communicate well-being and physical loadout. Equipment changes appearance, and picking up a weapon equips it directly. A carrying list may document items, but there is no abstract slot-rearrangement workflow.

### Character information and task priority

Show expressed Traits (including preferences), Needs, Shards, and discovered Interactions. Explain why known interactions exist and mark missing requirements or injury blockers. Known interactions remain documented when unusable.

Latent youth Traits including preferences and undiscovered interactions are completely hidden. No silhouettes, completion counts, or hints about undiscovered combinations. The panel is optional understanding and flavor, not required inspection during an action.

### Direct contextual inputs

Approaching a fountain or other target exposes available actions near the cursor or character. The chosen input directly performs the action and its animation. At a fountain, Exchange Knowledge and Accept Task are example separate actions. A subtle UI glow may invite optional information, without automatically opening a menu.

Right click moves; holding left click opens contextual radial actions. Mouse drag/release or displayed quick keys select an action. Exact quick-key mapping and maximum options remain open. The latest panel also allows selecting an existing task or quest priority, as described below.

### Navigation and selection

Divine Orientation uses subtle intermittent signs, not markers or arrows. Selection and queue recaps explain the known life without spoiling its unrevealed role or destiny. No player voice or text chat is introduced.

See [Interaction Discovery](../06_Gameplay/Interactions_Discovery_and_Player_Skill.md) and [Environmental Guidance](#environmental-guidance).

## Environmental Guidance

### Divine Orientation

Guidance is a divine influence: the gods can see the world's larger state and guide toward the next useful step. If a target moves or an item changes hands, orientation can redirect toward a current valid path.

This is distinct from personal and collective knowledge. Shards still spread locally through actual interactions and can be stale. Divine signs need not wait for a personal knowledge update; they do not automatically rewrite the person's Shards.

The gods suggest where to go next, not how to succeed. Travel, observation, discovery, interaction, and execution remain the player's activity.

### Subtle and intermittent

Signs are always part of the world but are not continuously firing. They are quiet, low-stimulation, sometimes absent for a while, and require attention. Players learn to recognize the world's language through experience. A contrary wind can suggest they drifted off course.

Wind, grass, light, birds, elemental resonance, and small environmental changes can express direction. A slight feeling of swiftness in the right direction is an optional example, not a fixed speed bonus. Random intervals were suggested, not specified.

No map markers, compass arrows, precision tiers, or constant prompts. Guidance avoids pointless searching for where the objective exists while leaving the journey full of discovery.

### First version and open question

One unified Divine Orientation system supports the first Grimnir settlement. Different gods' specific sign styles are deferred; the pantheon stays partly mysterious.

Accepted tasks and active Needs have guidance. Answer 105 additionally permits subtle nearby-opportunity hints for other threads or people; see the detailed rule below.

See [Diegetic Interface](#diegetic-interface) and [Knowledge Shards](../05_Knowledge/Shards_Memory_and_Cultural_Hubs.md).

## Selecting the active task and ordering its Needs

The panel now permits selecting an existing task or quest thread as the priority for Divine Orientation. This explicitly revises the earlier informational-only restriction; acquiring a new task and performing actions remain in-world interactions.

Clicking a thread activates it and marks it visibly. The chosen presentation is a marker stone on a seamlessly shaped notched stone board; exact artwork remains to be made. Its Needs are pursued one at a time; where several next Needs are valid, guidance may favor the nearest sensible one. On completion, the game can suggest the oldest unresolved task/Need, using oldest personal Need first and community fallback when none remain. The player can always choose another priority.

The panel still documents expressed Traits (including preferences), Needs, Shards, and discovered interactions, including blockers. It does not preview undiscovered combinations or provide inventory rearrangement. The contextual radial menu shows current executable actions only.

Source: [answer 109](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-6925b17c-b666-43cf-be30-828048602e2f), [answer 137](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-524f5a34-8d68-471d-94ca-a08337749441), [answer 138](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-bbb217ed-47f1-404c-aa1f-86eca788caca).



## Nearby opportunities and readable natural cues

Divine Orientation may gently draw attention to something nearby that helps a different Destiny thread, side quest, or nearby person's task, even while another mission is active. This can lead the player onto an unexpected path; it does not force a priority switch or automatically update mortal knowledge. The earlier Question 105 is answered by this nearby-opportunity behavior; later answers establish that one Need is always prioritized, with common-slot fallback.

Interaction discovery uses specific brief environmental events rather than outlines, universal glitter, or constant cursor sweeping. Examples: sunlight refracts off glass; wind bends grass or branches toward a spot; leaves drift toward a target as it enters view; a bird briefly lands on a tree; a falling leaf marks an interesting patch of ground. Nature or magic should be noticeable through attention and learned familiarity. Hover confirms current possibilities; deliberate hold opens focus. Preserve the quiet intermittent language of guidance.

Source: [answer 105](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-1e535e9f-6315-4c3a-b24b-04823b7cb312), [answer 126](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-a3e6d0a2-b40d-41b1-a443-b7cbfece9743).


## Always-active guidance and one shared sign language

Divine Orientation always has a prioritized target. Completing a Need advances to the next Need in its task/quest. Once the thread completes, select the oldest unresolved personal Need by default; if none exists, the common slot can take a suitable community Need. The player can override by moving the marker. This replaces an unanswered idle-guidance mode; signs can still be subtle, intermittent, and sometimes silent without losing the active target.

The same learned environmental guidance applies to everyone. No alternate brighter arrows, outlines, or GPS assistance is selected. This resolves the proposed special guidance-assistance mode; it does not define every other accessibility setting.


Source: [answer 177](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-b6e4e6a7-89e3-43e1-b4f4-d9020abe3078), [answer 178](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-5b7053e6-e763-4800-aad2-296d0c392a07), [answer 202](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-e9d1aed9-22e6-4c80-ab1b-dc4a8a1e2e0c).


## Character panel and stone Destiny board

The person screen has a body silhouette at top left showing injured body parts and equipped/carrying item slots beneath. Right-side tabs list Traits (including preferences), Needs, Shards, States, and discovered Interactions. Entries have names/descriptions; hovering can explain associated interactions or contributing reasons. Clicking the silhouette opens the Tasks and Destiny view.

The Destiny view resembles a seamlessly shaped stone tabletop game with branching lines and notched slots. One common task slot sits left; the Destiny side begins with four slots. The marker stone defaults to the common slot and can be moved to any unlocked quest slot to activate it. Hover may provide a small animation. Selecting/inspecting the stone opens a stone-disk close-up of the quest, listing Needs/objectives and the prioritized one at the top. Exact automatic-open versus click-to-open choreography is to be finalized.

The current Crest layout is **4 → 8 → 16 → 8 → 4 → 1 Knot**. This is now an explicit requested structure, replacing earlier statements that all counts were merely illustrations. It widens choices then narrows toward the Knot, whose completion opens the next Crest. Immediate upcoming slots can be visible while locked; the prerequisite beneath them must complete before the marker can move there. Do not expose the entire unrevealed future life.

The common task area expands into several personal/community tasks (healing is an example). The player selects one for the same priority stone used by Destiny. Oldest-personal then community fallback applies when a default is needed; a new important Need never overrides a current voluntary choice. Destiny quests retain their own internal objectives.


Source: [answer 199](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-e3fd3a18-f0ed-4edb-bed3-2b48c4372666), [answer 200](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-1da4c6eb-8f22-43a5-a97b-63ad950e2965), [answer 201](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-7d73a0bd-c7f2-480a-8817-36488ea081e7), [answer 202](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-e9d1aed9-22e6-4c80-ab1b-dc4a8a1e2e0c).


## Voluntary priority, important Needs and stone checklists

The common task area can hold several personal/community tasks. The lower-left compact display shows the selected task. Hover opens a small stone-style tooltip with its description; clicking opens the task list. Selecting a task moves the same priority stone, including away from a Destiny quest, and changes Divine Orientation. One selected target remains the guidance focus.

Urgent new Needs do not automatically seize that priority. Answer 233 initially selected automatic urgency; answer 234 explicitly replaces it with voluntary selection. The character-panel stone/button glows or pulses for important new information, and important task/Need entries are highlighted inside. The player can knowingly continue their current quest while injured. The panel button's exact screen position is not finalized; bottom-left was an example.

Importance is a configurable property of each hand-authored Need, tuned through content design and testing. Injury and sleep deprivation are examples. There is no runtime algorithm inventing an importance category or a mandatory confirmation popup. Initial/default task fallback can still choose the oldest personal Need and then community work when a replacement selection is needed; it must not override an ongoing player choice because a new important Need appeared.

The panel has Traits (including preferences), Needs, Shards, States and discovered Interactions; remove the separate Desires tab. Own known details and spectator character inspection do not authorize inspecting strangers. In-world names/healthbars/role badges/relationship icons are absent; Recall Knowledge shows learned person-specific detail on a stone disk.

Tasks show a small requirement list. Unknown information is a step to acquire information at the Memory Fountain, then becomes the learned requirement/location after exchange. See [task lifecycle](../06_Gameplay/Needs_Tasks_and_Solution_Lifecycle.md). These lists prioritize already existing tasks; accepting new community work still happens through the cultural source interaction.
