# Identity Traits Desires and Needs

Temporary states are distinct from permanent traits, can coexist, and can modify simple values as well as actions. See [States and buffs](States_Buffs_and_Visible_Condition.md).

**Current concept through answer 202 · updated 2026-09-12.**

The complete character vocabulary: persistent Traits and Desires, situational Needs, and the generation rules that assemble a person. Shards have their own chapter under Knowledge.

## Character System

### World-owned lives

Every playable character already belongs to the persistent world and community. They alternate between AI and player control. A later player continues that person's actual history, rather than resetting an avatar.

### Identity

Traits provide the building blocks for abilities, personality, appearance, and eventually social role and destiny. Desires add persistent direction; Needs and Shards provide situational requirements and knowledge. Strength should visibly affect form; the person should look and act like the qualities that compose them. Family and social connections may provide a starting network, although relationship detail is not settled.

Characters also carry current knowledge, objects, life stage, ongoing objectives, and embodied well-being. Knowledge shards provide situational information and should not be confused with permanently acquired traits.

### Life course

Birth creates 27 latent traits. Early childhood is simulated. Playable youth reveals hidden qualities through participation; a final ritual reveals role and begins adulthood and destiny. Players advance destiny, while AI maintains ordinary life. Exhaustion of godly essence leads to Atranor and possible continuation or dissolution.

See [Traits](#traits), [Childhood](Birth_Youth_and_Adulthood.md), and [Adulthood](Birth_Youth_and_Adulthood.md).

The character now has four explicit conceptual sets: Traits, Desires, Needs, and Shards. There are 27 target Desires with inherited, birth-generated, and experiential origins. Universal humanoid actions and elemental abilities provide a baseline modified by personal qualities, role, equipment, and injuries. See [Desires](#desires), [Needs](#needs), and [Baseline Actions](Roles_and_Baseline_Abilities.md).

## Traits

### Rules

Traits are discrete qualities: a character either has one or does not. There are no trait ranks in the current design. Progress adds diversity and new possibilities rather than increasingly narrow vertical specialization.

Once acquired, a trait remains. No forgetting, confidence decay, or loss of competence is imposed as routine upkeep. Temporary fatigue and changes in visible well-being do not delete underlying traits.

### Breadth

The four broad families are Combat, Trickery, Aid, and Social. Qualities can be physical, practical, social, magical, or knowledge-related. Examples from the discussion include Strong, Funny, Loved, Fast, Good Climber, Good Speaker, Green Thumb, Knows Herbs, Knows Language, Knows Element, and Knows Magic. This is not a finalized trait catalogue.

### Acquisition

A newborn has 27 latent traits: 9 drawn from each parent and 9 from the complete pool. Participation during playable youth reveals the remaining latent qualities. Side quests can add further traits based on their activities, such as Climber from climbing or Knows Herbs from studying plants. The 27 count is the birth pool, not a lifetime cap.

### Knowledge distinction

Traits and Desires are persistent; Shards and Needs are situational. Sharing duplicates knowledge. Receiving a newer relevant shard replaces an obsolete personal shard; collective memory retains historical layers. There is no shard burden or capacity system. This distinction does not change the permanence of acquired personal traits.

Desires have defined trait counterparts. Characters may possess the very trait they desire. Traits also describe world entities, but those current-state traits can change through world events and repopulation; the permanent personal-trait rule must not be applied indiscriminately to a cave's danger or a node's occupant.

Shared humanoid and elemental baseline capabilities may be represented as underlying traits. They are not specified as consuming the 27 personal birth-trait slots. Injuries can restrict actions without deleting the underlying permanent trait. See [Baseline Actions](Roles_and_Baseline_Abilities.md), [Desires](#desires), and [World Nodes](../01_World/World_Nodes_and_Generation.md).

### Crafting and conditional capability

Applicable crafting traits stack horizontally and can be inherited, generated, or earned. Their contribution to crafting is deterministic. Equipment can expose conditional interaction capabilities; these should not be mistaken for new permanent personal traits that survive losing the item. See [Crafting](../07_Items/Crafting_and_Item_Traits.md).

## Desires

Desires are persistent directions paired with Traits in people or the wider world. A person may desire a quality they already possess. The counterpart can be on a creature, object, garment, tool, or place, as well as another character.

### Origin and expression

The target remains 27 Desires: 9 inherited in total, 9 generated at birth, and 9 mostly random youth-emergent Desires. Exact parental selection remains open. Latent desires stay hidden until they emerge. Playable youth reveals or develops them through tasks and ordinary participation, completing youth development before the final adulthood rite.

### Generation pressure

The design vocabulary gives every Desire a matching Trait, but a matching instance need not exist immediately. If suitable world content can answer an unmet Desire, generate or prioritize it. If the counterpart requires a person, prioritize that trait in future newborn generation rather than rewriting an existing person.

This influence is local to the settlement for now. It does not manipulate distant cultures. Desires can remain unmatched while the world develops toward a possible counterpart.

This is a refinement of completely unconstrained random generation: the 27-trait structure and absence of category balancing remain, but future traits can have local desire-based priority. Exact weighting and which draw it affects are open.

### Play

Desires shape social matches, destiny possibilities, and equipment compatibility. More item traits create more potential matches, not universal superiority. Compatibility primarily opens interactions rather than changing hidden success rolls.

See [Trait Generation](#trait-generation), [Crafting](../07_Items/Crafting_and_Item_Traits.md), and [Interaction System](../06_Gameplay/Interactions_Discovery_and_Player_Skill.md).

## Needs

### Situational requirements

Needs arise from quests, side quests, community tasks, exchanges, and changed circumstances. Persistent Desires seek Traits; situational Needs connect with Shards and actionable solutions.

### A solution must exist

When a Need is created, the system checks for a possible solution. If it exists, connect to it; otherwise generate what makes resolution possible. This does not guarantee immediate success or reveal every step to the player.

Failure can create a treatment Need, a replacement-tool Need, a search for an escaped creature, or another recovery requirement. Failure changes the route without permanently closing it.

An exchange may require meeting another person's Need or Desire first. The resulting chain has no fixed depth cap and can be worked back through after a match is found.

### Acting on a Need

The character panel explains Needs and relevant Shards and allows selection of an existing task or quest priority. Accepting a new task remains an in-world action. Divine Orientation follows the chosen thread’s current Need; the player can switch priorities.

The knowledge fountain supplies suitable community work and relevant knowledge through direct cultural interactions. Divine Orientation guides toward the next useful step, even when current reality changes independently of personal knowledge.

A task/Need is always prioritized; the common slot defaults to oldest personal Need and then a community Need. Nearby-opportunity hints remain allowed.

See [Community Tasks](../04_Community/Settlement_Simulation_and_Tasks.md), [Environmental Guidance](../08_Presentation/Interface_and_Divine_Orientation.md), and [Trade and Exchange](../07_Items/Trade_and_Exchange.md).

## Trait Generation

### Personal traits

The 27 birth-trait structure remains: 9 drawn from each parent and 9 from the full pool. Do not impose a balanced Combat/Trickery/Aid/Social spread or generate a predetermined role first.

### Local desire priority

Completely unconstrained randomness is refined: an unmet Desire whose counterpart must be a person can prioritize the matching trait in future newborns. Existing people are not overwritten. Influence stays local to the settlement.

How priority affects the parental versus global draws, its weights, and distinct-draw/reroll handling remain open; duplicate Traits do not stack. The rule does not guarantee immediate fulfillment or rewrite the 9+9+9 counts.

### Expression and growth

Early childhood is simulated. Youth is playable with all elemental baseline abilities and some expressed personal qualities. Latent traits and desires are hidden until revealed through activity. Completing youth development and the final ritual leads to adulthood, role revelation, and destiny beginning.

Permanent personal traits accumulate through relevant quests; 27 is not a lifetime cap. Flat crafting traits may each be inherited, generated, or earned, and applicable ones contribute together.

Desires have a separate target of 9 inherited total, 9 birth-generated, and 9 mostly random youth-emergent. Their parental split and precise acquisition rules remain open. Baseline and equipment-dependent capabilities are not specified as consuming personal birth slots.

See [Desires](#desires), [Childhood](Birth_Youth_and_Adulthood.md), and [Crafting](../07_Items/Crafting_and_Item_Traits.md).

## Exact matching, duplicate Traits, and independent Needs

Desire–Trait and Need–Shard matches are exact. Related qualities do not automatically substitute for the required counterpart. Finding Kind when one desires Kind should be significant; the design does not broaden it into any vaguely helpful quality.

Traits are presence, not stacks: **Kind + Kind = Kind**. Duplicate sources do not improve a quality, rank it up, or unlock another interaction. An item remains a distinct physical object with its own traits, but character compatibility checks only require presence. Source tracking when temporary item effects end still needs specification. Duplicate draw handling does not yet establish 27 distinct effective traits at birth.

Each Need has its own completion. If two Needs concern the same person, place, Shard, or object, resolving one does not automatically resolve both. Repeated consumable requirements require sufficient separate supply; no accidental double-spending of the same meat or item. Tasks usually group Needs, while basic social, cultural, food, and rest Needs can exist outside quests.

Source: [answer 134](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-771a5841-da17-4a04-8962-a1f8e604fb52), [answer 135](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-9615656b-8fa1-43aa-83ce-0fdeb9ad8c58), [answer 136](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-fb75fc58-50fa-4451-9d66-e01e89498843), [answer 137](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-524f5a34-8d68-471d-94ca-a08337749441), [answer 147](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-bbb21991-6ffc-4722-86c5-a8f5f8c87246).



## Guaranteed fulfillment without manufactured conflict

Each generated Need points to or causes a fitting attainable fulfillment. Do not manufacture mutually exclusive demands on the same unique object: an intact-item Need and a break-item Need receive different suitable instances, including an already broken version or another breakable object.

Dependency chains may expand while active, but must not return to their own unresolved requirements. If A requires B, B requires C, and C would require A, choose a different fulfillment for C. If A and C both require meat, provide enough separate portions. The challenge is the journey and skillful interactions, not an impossible generation puzzle. Existing world content can still be reused where these guarantees hold; this is not permission to erase causal item history.

Source: [answer 139](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-bbb21aa5-8457-4739-bea1-0d29a88d83ea), [answer 147](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-bbb21991-6ffc-4722-86c5-a8f5f8c87246).
