# Shards, Memory and Cultural Hubs

**Current concept through answer 154 · updated 2026-09-11.**

This chapter brings the entire knowledge system together: what a Shard is, how people learn and update information, where history lives, and how each culture makes knowledge useful.

## Knowledge Shards

Shards are functional, situational information that enables an action or interaction. A bear sighting, an object's owner, a useful cave resource, and a relationship fact can all matter. They are not a general autobiography or diary. Broad permanent expertise such as Knows Herbs remains a Trait; the current location of a bear is a Shard.

The design pairs Need ↔ Shard, alongside persistent Desire ↔ Trait. A Need has a possible solution in the world, but the character need not already possess its answer.

### Collection and direct sharing

Characters collect encountered knowledge without capacity limits, per-shard weight, burden, or oldest-first eviction. The brief proposals for those mechanics were explicitly dropped. “Full to the brim” in a later description does not reinstate them.

Person-to-person sharing duplicates knowledge: the source retains it. Character exchanges require proximity and a performed cultural interaction; simply standing together does not synchronize information.

### Newer knowledge replaces stale personal knowledge

A character can carry an outdated report until they receive newer relevant information from another person or the cultural hub. A newer same-node shard creates an exchange opportunity. After the interaction supplies the new knowledge, the obsolete personal shard is discarded and replaced.

This is correction of an outdated fact, not general forgetting or erosion of permanent Traits. The world changing alone does not update personal knowledge.

## Collective Memory

The communal archive holds knowledge its people have learned and shared, including historical layers. It is not a global omniscient database.

Older records remain alongside newer ones. For a current Need, the hub selects the newest relevant information it has. A character discards obsolete personal information upon replacement; the collective archive preserves it as history.

A world node maintains its current geography and contents, not a local diary. On repopulation its old node-associated shard-state disappears, while copies held by people or collective memory remain. The memory of a former bear survives through those copies even after the cave has another occupant.

### Contribution without memory management

Visiting a hub enriches communal knowledge. The final simplification was summarized in the conversation as contributing copies; it does not establish a mandatory personal wipe. The earlier act of offloading in order to lighten oneself is no longer a required mechanic. Whether contribution also offers optional personal clearing remains unspecified.

## Cultural Knowledge Systems

The user supplied an extract from the broader worldbuilding project identifying these foundations:

| People | Existing knowledge tradition | Practical extension for this RPG |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verdani | Memory Pools, Memorykeepers, experiences preserved in enchanted water. | Consult or contribute relevant memories of places, creatures, and objects. |
| Rokur | Clan-Hearths as historical/educational centers; Hearthkeepers and storytellers. | Share reports through cultural storytelling or connect with a knowledgeable person. |
| Aevis | Crystal archives, harmonic chambers, Resonance Archives, communal repositories. | Retrieve observations and descriptions through resonance. |
| Grimnir | Memory stones, architectural and craft records, Memory-Keepers, traveling Skykeepers. | Consult stones and keepers, trace objects through makers, and exchange reports between communities. |

These are broader lore foundations. Their exact primitive forms still need design, and a dedicated Grimnir consultation space was proposed as an extension. Begin with the Grimnir system for the first settlement.

Small communities know fewer reports; larger archives and traveling keepers can broaden available information. Descriptions can be incomplete and sightings old. The archive knows only what has reached it.

## Memory Wells and Knowledge Fountains

These names refer to the cultural hub function, not a requirement for every people to construct the same watery object. Its main gameplay purpose is useful knowledge and task support, not browsing a comprehensive history library.

Approach the hub and perform the appropriate ritual or contextual action. It supplies relevant knowledge directly. A missing shard is a reason to consult the hub or a person, not a requirement to use a free-text query or player chat.

### Updating a route

If an old record places a bear at a cave, recorded later events may reveal who killed it and who now carries its claw. The hub follows the knowledge it holds and supplies the newest relevant lead. The objective can then point toward the holder and an exchange.

The lookup happens within the cultural consultation. It does not automatically learn unobserved events or telepathically update everyone.

### Providing community tasks

The fountain also supplies suitable work generated by the settlement. Exchange Knowledge and Accept Task can be separate direct contextual actions. A task fits the person's capabilities and current communal needs, or useful preparation if no urgent work fits. A cultural animation performs the action without opening an activity-selection menu.

See [Settlement Simulation and Tasks](../04_Community/Settlement_Simulation_and_Tasks.md) for cooperation and task adaptation.

## Knowledge as Gameplay

Explore → acquire functional knowledge → meet someone or visit a hub → perform an exchange → act on the updated situation → bring useful discoveries back.

The character panel explains known Needs and relevant Shards. Choices are performed in the world. A holder's requirement can create another Need and an exchange chain; ordinary information sharing is not automatically a paid transaction.

### Divine Orientation is separate

The gods can guide toward the current useful step even when mortal knowledge is stale. Those signs do not rewrite the character's Shards or make the cultural archive omniscient. The player still has to observe, travel, interact, and succeed.

See [Interface and Divine Orientation](../08_Presentation/Interface_and_Divine_Orientation.md) and [Trade and Exchange](../07_Items/Trade_and_Exchange.md).

## Remaining details

Same-node topic identification, timestamps or versioning, conflicting reports, exact contribution behavior, and retrieval mechanics remain open. No shard-quality ranking, guidance-strength tier, or memory-capacity management is required. Historical investigation may be revisited, but it is not the initial hub's main activity.

## Accurate observation and cultural exchange

An observed Shard accurately records what was actually witnessed. Do not add perception rolls, false memories, or misunderstood observations. Accuracy at observation does not make knowledge current forever.

A newer report updates personal practical knowledge only through an actual Exchange Knowledge interaction or interaction with the cultural source. If one person knows the bear was at the river and another knows it moved to the cave, the newer counterpart makes exchange available; performing it replaces the outdated practical information. Collective historical layers remain as already established.

Exchange Knowledge is important Grimnir conversation about life, including making things and who has them, not only quest coordinates. A crafter gains a Shard recording that they made a particular spear. Others learn it by exchanging with the crafter or someone informed, or by interacting with the communal source after contribution. Depositing it makes it communally available, not automatically known by every character.

Seeing or picking up that spear does not reveal its maker. If relevant item knowledge exists, encountering it can create the paired Need: “Who made this spear?” or “Whose spear is this?” The answering Shard may be with the crafter, an informed person, or the cultural source. Identification creates a small social discovery thread. The latest discussion explicitly includes provenance and lived events as functional knowledge; avoid interpreting the older 'not diaries' phrase as excluding them.
