# Interface and Divine Orientation

**Current concept through answer 154 · updated 2026-09-11.**

What the player can inspect, how actions are performed directly, and how divine signs guide without becoming a constant pointer.

## Diegetic Interface

### Explain the person; act in the world

The major experience choice happens when inhabiting a character. The player accepts that person's role and then lives it through world actions. The UI explains identity and known possibilities and permits prioritizing existing tasks; physical actions are chosen through contextual focus.

Bodies communicate well-being and physical loadout. Equipment changes appearance, and picking up a weapon equips it directly. A carrying list may document items, but there is no abstract slot-rearrangement workflow.

### Character information and task priority

Show expressed Traits, Desires, Needs, Shards, and discovered Interactions. Explain why known interactions exist and mark missing requirements or injury blockers. Known interactions remain documented when unusable.

Latent youth traits/desires and undiscovered interactions are completely hidden. No silhouettes, completion counts, or hints about undiscovered combinations. The panel is optional understanding and flavor, not required inspection during an action.

### Direct contextual inputs

Approaching a fountain or other target exposes available actions near the cursor or character. The chosen input directly performs the action and its animation. At a fountain, Exchange Knowledge and Accept Task are example separate actions. A subtle UI glow may invite optional information, without automatically opening a menu.

Right click moves; holding left click opens contextual radial actions. Mouse drag/release or displayed quick keys select an action. Exact quick-key mapping and maximum options remain open. The latest panel also allows selecting an existing task or quest priority, as described below.

### Navigation and selection

Divine Orientation uses subtle intermittent signs, not markers or arrows. Selection and queue recaps explain the known life without spoiling its unrevealed role or destiny. No player voice or text chat is introduced.

See [Interaction Discovery](../06_Gameplay/Interactions_Discovery_and_Player_Skill.md) and [Environmental Guidance](#environmental-guidance).

## Environmental Guidance

### Divine Orientation

Guidance is a divine influence: the gods can see the world's larger state and guide toward the next useful step. If a target moves or an item changes hands, orientation can redirect toward a current valid path.

This is distinct from personal and collective knowledge. Shards still spread locally through actual interactions and can be stale. Divine signs need not wait for a personal knowledge update; they do not automatically rewrite the person's Shards.

The gods suggest where to go next, not how to succeed. Travel, observation, discovery, interaction, and execution remain the player's activity.

### Subtle and intermittent

Signs are always part of the world but are not continuously firing. They are quiet, low-stimulation, sometimes absent for a while, and require attention. Players learn to recognize the world's language through experience. A contrary wind can suggest they drifted off course.

Wind, grass, light, birds, elemental resonance, and small environmental changes can express direction. A slight feeling of swiftness in the right direction is an optional example, not a fixed speed bonus. Random intervals were suggested, not specified.

No map markers, compass arrows, precision tiers, or constant prompts. Guidance avoids pointless searching for where the objective exists while leaving the journey full of discovery.

### First version and open question

One unified Divine Orientation system supports the first Grimnir settlement. Different gods' specific sign styles are deferred; the pantheon stays partly mysterious.

Accepted tasks and active Needs have guidance. Answer 105 additionally permits subtle nearby-opportunity hints for other threads or people; see the detailed rule below.

See [Diegetic Interface](#diegetic-interface) and [Knowledge Shards](../05_Knowledge/Shards_Memory_and_Cultural_Hubs.md).

## Selecting the active task and ordering its Needs

The panel now permits selecting an existing task or quest thread as the priority for Divine Orientation. This explicitly revises the earlier informational-only restriction; acquiring a new task and performing actions remain in-world interactions.

Clicking a thread activates it and marks it visibly. A movable stone on a board is the proposed cultural visual metaphor, not a finalized asset. Its Needs are pursued one at a time; where several next Needs are valid, guidance may favor the nearest sensible one. On completion, the game can suggest the oldest unresolved task/Need, with urgency also mentioned but no formal ranking specified. The player can always choose another priority.

The panel still documents expressed traits, desires, Needs, Shards, and discovered interactions, including blockers. It does not preview undiscovered combinations or provide inventory rearrangement. The contextual radial menu shows current executable actions only.

## Nearby opportunities and readable natural cues

Divine Orientation may gently draw attention to something nearby that helps a different Destiny thread, side quest, or nearby person's task, even while another mission is active. This can lead the player onto an unexpected path; it does not force a priority switch or automatically update mortal knowledge. The earlier Question 105 is answered by this nearby-opportunity behavior; a universal idle-guidance algorithm is not specified.

Interaction discovery uses specific brief environmental events rather than outlines, universal glitter, or constant cursor sweeping. Examples: sunlight refracts off glass; wind bends grass or branches toward a spot; leaves drift toward a target as it enters view; a bird briefly lands on a tree; a falling leaf marks an interesting patch of ground. Nature or magic should be noticeable through attention and learned familiarity. Hover confirms current possibilities; deliberate hold opens focus. Preserve the quiet intermittent language of guidance.
