# Combat and Injuries

Combat is a live alternating exchange of authored actions and responses. Each participant's capabilities, equipment, body and circumstances determine what they can do.

## Engagement

An attack requires a valid opponent, suitable capability and a meaningful encounter context, such as a resource, knowledge or task objective. Focus opens the available actions. While engaged, the game manages approach, facing and positioning so the participants remain oriented toward one another.

The player disengages to move independently or flee. After one action and its response, the opponent receives their action opportunity.

## Mountain Strike

1. Hold left mouse on the creature to focus. The character and target slow while contextual choices emerge.
2. Select Mountain Strike. The character quickly approaches the target.
3. A small body diagram near the cursor shows the creature's head, torso and limbs.
4. Select the body part to attack and click at the correct moment.
5. Resolve the attack and any available defense, then continue the exchange.

The skill display begins during approach. Failing to click wastes the action; missing the timing produces an unsuccessful attack. Mountain Strike requires a usable arm.

## Body-part consequences

A successful unblocked attack injures the selected part. Combat has no numerical HP layer. Body parts are equally hittable by default; the target's available defenses affect the outcome.

| Part | Functional consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Arm or claw | Actions requiring that limb become unavailable. |
| Leg | Running or escape actions that depend on it are restricted. |
| Torso | A hit can stun the target for the next round. |
| Head | A hit can cause knockout. |

Each creature's authored actions use its anatomy. A bear with one injured claw can still use an action supported by the other healthy claw. Injuries change capability in the encounter while permanent Traits remain.

## Defense and attack reading

Stoneborn Resilience focuses reinforcement on a body region. Selecting the attacked region and executing the defense can absorb or block the attack. Another defensive interaction can provide a different response where its requirements fit.

Reading the opponent's target is a separate learned capability. A character with the appropriate Trait can receive a cue identifying the threatened part. Otherwise, the player reads the motion and chooses their defensive region from the encounter's visible information.

## Incapacity and recovery

As injury or exhaustion closes actions, other contextual possibilities can become relevant: crawling away, surrender, help, capture, protecting someone, reaching safety, playing dead or a desperate response.

AI creatures follow the same functional-state principle. Their condition can open opportunities to spare, capture or assist them.

Treatment and repair create Needs leading to appropriate knowledge, items, people and interactions. Failed actions can be attempted again when their conditions and the next action opportunity permit.

## Facing the bear

The player focuses a bear and chooses Mountain Strike. During approach, they target a leg and complete the timing challenge. If the bear's defense does not stop the strike, the injured leg restricts its dependent movement. On the bear's action, the player chooses a defense supported by their own Traits and healthy body parts.
