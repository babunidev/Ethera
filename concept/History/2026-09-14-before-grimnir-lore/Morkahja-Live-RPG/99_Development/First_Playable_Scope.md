# First Playable Scope

**Current concept through answer 251 · updated 2026-09-13.**

Consolidated concept material, with current rules and unresolved details distinguished.

## One Grimnir settlement first

Build one functioning settlement and one culture before expanding to the other peoples. The chosen starting culture is earth-aligned Grimnir. This replaces the assumption that four settlements must ship together.

The other peoples remain part of Morkahja's broader lore and longer-term design. They have not yet met in this ancient era. Local generation priorities do not reach other cultures for now.

## What the experience should prove

A living autonomous community; simulated early childhood and playable youth; participation-driven development; a coming-of-age ritual revealing role and beginning destiny; cultural knowledge and useful tasks; a procedural local world; meaningful interactions and persistent objects.

This is a concept focus, not an approved implementation milestone checklist or a promise to ship every system at once.

## Emotional direction

Mystery, wonder, curiosity, and a dreamlike low-stimulation sense of a great civilization just beginning. Grimnir are emerging from mindless stone or earth existence into individuals and community. The beginning feels significant without loud spectacle.

Detailed architecture and role rosters remain open. Grimnir nourishment, rest, reproduction, elemental baseline, and early culture now have dedicated detail in the character chapters. Hunter examples are not a required role roster.

One unified Divine Orientation system comes first. Identifying individual gods through distinct guidance styles is a later idea. The working starting population is 30 Grimnir, subject to refinement.

See [Grimnir](../02_Peoples/Grimnir.md) and [Game Vision](../00_Core/Game_Vision_and_Principles.md).

## Concrete first playable and society target

Use 30 Grimnir as the initial settlement target, refining it against resources, roles, and tasks. The earliest test can use only a few simple characters sharing one architecture, with their own IDs and slightly different matching Traits including preferences. Switching between them through spectator mode is part of the requested prototype: leave one person to AI, observe, and inhabit another. This does not finalize unrestricted switching during production combat or remove established logout rules.

The first successful slice may be a moving blob with unanimated placeholder graphics: boxes marked House, lines marked Wooden Wall, or even black-and-white drawings. Preserve a hint of natural savanna atmosphere through space, grass, wind, and simple cues.

Build in small steps toward moving through the village, greeting someone, picking up and visibly carrying/equipping an item, discovering a landmark or stone circle, gaining a Shard, returning it to the cultural source, and unlocking one interaction using one or two traits and/or an item. A climbable tree, elementary Divine Orientation, and the small Traits/Needs/Shards model are illustrative early targets, not a demand to finish every system at once. A temporary hello bubble is acceptable for testing despite the final no-player-chat direction.

On entering the ongoing world, a character usually already has a task and adults an available Destiny thread. Continue it or choose another existing thread in the panel. A separate scripted onboarding sequence is not required by this discussion. The desired proof is that inhabitation, discovery, carrying, knowledge, and compatibility interact meaningfully, then grow step by step.

## Selected first content and technical foundation

Build toward twelve authored Grimnir interactions (three per branch), their matching Needs, body-part combat, Shard-backed task guidance and inhabitable shared entities. The earliest movement/interaction test can remain small and use placeholders. The twelve are a content target, not a claim every interaction recipe or animation has been finished. A common entity/interaction framework and flat Trait matching support the game; RealmNode remains the separate distributed runtime. Engine choice, content-editor implementation and network authority have not been chosen by these answers. See [first twelve](../06_Gameplay/First_Twelve_Grimnir_Interactions.md) and [architecture](../09_Technical/Entities_Interactions_and_Content_Authoring.md).
