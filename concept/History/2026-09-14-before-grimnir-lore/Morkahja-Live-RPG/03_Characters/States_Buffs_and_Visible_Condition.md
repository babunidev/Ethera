## Temporary effects, outing uses, and body presentation

Positive states can enable interactions and directly modify simple properties. Examples explicitly discussed: Swiftness (movement speed), Sharpness (view distance), and longer quick-time-event windows. Suggested five or ten percent bonuses were examples, not final balance. Extra examples in assistant responses, such as special combinations of Well Fed and Rested, are not accepted new mechanics.

More compatible participants in a social event extend its buff duration rather than automatically increasing magnitude. Everyone physically in the village when the event happens can receive the effect, including nonparticipants; travelers outside do not receive it remotely.

Different effect types coexist. The same type does not stack numerically: the better/longer remaining effect replaces or refreshes the shorter one. Exact comparison when magnitude and remaining uses disagree remains open.

Duration is measured by outings rather than elapsed minutes. A one-use buff lasts through departure and the adventure, then is consumed immediately upon re-entering the settlement—even after a short trip, without resting or performing a ritual. Larger events may give two or three outing uses; each return consumes one and the buff ends after its last use. Exact participant thresholds, settlement boundaries, and non-travel state lifetimes remain unspecified.

People communicate village condition through posture, gestures, movement, health, clothing, happiness, or weakness; no abstract village-happiness meter is needed. For overlapping visible states, show the oldest still-active one first. Example: Tired begins, then Injured; show tiredness until rested, then show injury. This avoids layering multiple condition animations.

The display priority is visual only. All active states simultaneously affect mechanics and are listed in the panel. If several sources enable one interaction, availability remains binary with no stronger duplicate action. The tooltip can explain all enabling reasons: personal Trait, item, role, or state. Interaction prerequisite logic is not reduced to 'any one source' where a recipe requires a combination.
