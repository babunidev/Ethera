# Needs, alternatives and exact objects

**Current concept through answer 251 · updated 2026-09-13.**


## Needs, alternatives and exact objects

A Need is a structured object with an ID, owner, fulfillment requirements and relevant metadata, rather than merely a character tag. Task/quest origin, author-set importance, priority linkage and found solution references can support tasks and guidance. A hunger Need seeks suitable nourishment; a special quest can name a particular item or interaction.

Support multiple valid fulfillment paths: A OR B OR C. An item, a person with the right Trait, an appropriate Shard or a performed interaction may provide alternatives. A complex authored task can still require several separate things together. Do not confuse alternative solutions for one Need with the complete requirement groups of an interaction.

Most Needs describe matching requirements and search when needed. A specific Need can bind an exact entity, such as a particular relic. Generic fulfillment does not make unique identity interchangeable; losing a particular spear can still mean finding that spear.

Source: [answer 244](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-3319432e-9eb2-4394-8ae3-68e6dee3a1b4), [answer 245](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-40c3239f-4ed9-4e97-a85c-1f9836af5193), [answer 246](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-4d5977ef-ac7a-4cea-bcf1-11dcc82c647b).


## Resolve solutions when the fountain is consulted

The final lifecycle replaces the earlier rule that every Need immediately spawns or binds its solution:

1. A Need appears and is represented in the relevant task. A solution need not yet have been generated.
2. If the player already has supplies, knowledge, a crafting method or suitable help, they can pursue a valid solution directly.
3. When the character actively exchanges knowledge at the Memory Fountain, check the current Needs for free, valid existing solutions.
4. Reuse an available suitable solution. If none exists, create the needed content at a random valid loot-capable place somewhere in the world: a chest/container, creature loot or another authored loot source. No proximity optimization is selected.
5. Give the corresponding solution/location information as a Shard, then let Divine Orientation follow that knowledge into the journey and required interaction.

Example: if village storage contains fifteen salves and a free one fits the injury, no extra salve is needed. If no valid one is free, create a suitable solution in the world. A player who can craft the salve or knows a maker may use that alternative instead of traveling to the generated location.

The earlier absolute solvability intent remains, with deferred resolution/generation. Preserve acyclic prerequisites, sufficient consumable supply and separate fulfillment for separate Needs. Do not force preserve and destroy requirements onto the same unique object. How concurrent requests reserve supplies, keep routes attainable after theft/consumption, and handle exact-entity loss is still open.

Never teleport an existing quest item into another player's inventory as a reward. Someone may already be traveling to it. New fallback loot is random, not selected by bias toward unresolved Needs. World objects already discovered or obtained retain causal continuity, and prior deeds can satisfy suitable later tasks. Consultation-generated content does not erase previously existing content.

Source: [answer 231](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-b882f992-ab6f-4fc8-ae67-a56b9903ef71), [answer 232](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-da1625f1-823e-4990-9612-1646980a84b7), [answer 233](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-e1eba182-03cb-4ae5-9f77-d6532268a73e), [answer 234](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-b38ca86d-0142-4d9c-bccd-e5c69f1e3521), [answer 247](../../Morkahja-Concept-Sources.md#turn-5d52a625-c52d-4a5f-a31b-06bbb1ffea9a).


## A Shard-backed task checklist

The current structure is Need → information Shard → item/person/place → authored interaction. A task exposes a small to-do list of its requirements. It is not simply an item pickup objective, and the checklist does not complete a treatment merely because an ingredient was obtained.

Healing example: acquire information about a healing staff, find the staff, find someone with a healing capability, combine the required staff/Traits/tools/states, perform the healing skill phase, then resolve the injury Need. Salve, healer or a place can be other authored solution designs; not every treatment must use the same staff recipe.

If a requirement or its location is not yet known, show “Acquire information at the Memory Fountain.” After knowledge exchange, replace that unknown step with the learned requirement/destination. Do not reveal information the character has not learned. Players who already know the requirements and locations can proceed themselves.

Answer 248 ends by choosing the direct Shard bridge: when the fountain finds/creates the solution, supply its identifying/location Shard rather than bypass knowledge with an unexplained arrow. Earlier parts of that answer explored an extra journey to a Shard holder; the final preference is the direct bridge. This is a special task-resolution function, not global omniscience for ordinary collective history. Precisely how the new solution Shard is attributed when no mortal reported it remains to be clarified. Answer 249 permits a broader source/place interpretation but concentrates on the requirement chain; exact locator granularity remains content detail.

Question 252 remains unanswered: can requirements be completed in any order, or do tasks enforce logical dependencies? The information-first display is settled; do not infer a universal mandatory sequence or a forced fountain visit if the player already knows a valid route.
